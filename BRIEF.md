# Bouncy Car – Brief

_Work name. Drafted 2026-11-06._

## Pitch

Drive a big, floaty, indestructible-ish car down an endless procedurally generated snowy
mountain. Hit bumps, catch air, land hard, lose a bumper, keep going. The fun is in the
**feel**: soft suspension, body roll, big jumps and satisfying crunches.

## Pillars

1. **Feel first.** Driver-style (1999) handling: soft, bouncy suspension, visible body roll and
   pitch, forgiving arcade grip, controllable slides. If it isn't fun to drive on flat ground,
   nothing else matters.
2. **Terrain made for jumping.** Mostly downhill, bumpy, with rolling hills, moguls and natural
   kicker ridges running across the slope.
3. **Crunchy, but robust.** Cars dent and shed parts, but they're tougher than real cars, so a
   session lasts.
4. **Low-poly, nicely lit.** Flat-shaded geometry, soft sun shadows, fog for depth, a coherent
   palette. Polish comes from lighting and atmosphere, not polygon count.

| World | Endless chunk-streamed terrain from seeded noise. Downhill along −Z. The mountain is carved into valleys running roughly down the fall line, each wandering along its own line with a level floor and flanks rising up to ≈60 m (exaggerated, so they read against the slope) to ridges between them, which sink where two valleys converge; valleys deepen and shallow along their length, and some country is open broad slope. Gullies a few metres deep run down the flanks along the ground's own fall line, slantwise into the valleys, forking and joining, and shoulder-like ledges level the flanks off here and there. Rocky knolls stand about, more in rocky country and seldom on valley floors (4–8 m tall, about one per 17 ha, none near the start or a set piece): shaped like glacier-worn rock, a long gentle ramp on the uphill side to run up and a short steep rock face downhill to fly off, with steep rock sides. Landmarks to steer by stand where they're seen (never on a valley floor or near a set piece): on the very tops of cliffs and rocky knolls, about seven per km², a **varde**, a cairn of 7–10 flat stones stacked about 2–3 m tall with a few more leaning round its foot (snow-topped in the snow); its stones lie loose on each other, so any knock brings it down with a stony knock, the stones clacking on each other and thudding down as they tumble and settle with rock weight, and staying down; and rarely (about one per 3 km²) on a rise in the grassland a **great oak**, alone, ≈15 m tall and ≈20 m across, a massive twisting trunk whose roots grow out of it and run down into the ground, following the slope it stands on, splitting into big crooked limbs that carry the crown as separate rounded masses of chunky, faceted yellow-green leaves, high enough to drive under. The AI and traffic keep clear of both. Over this lie moguls and kicker ridges across the slope (about one in five bigger, for extra launch). Biomes change along the descent: a **snowy mountain** (snow everywhere you drive, shading to an icy blue on steep faces like drop walls; rock only on near-vertical faces) turning into a snowy forest, then from ≈3.5 km a **thaw** where the snow breaks up into patches that linger on flatter and shaded ground, and from ≈5 km **grassland**: grass (dry and brown just after the snow, greening further down; lusher in moist valley bottoms, a little drier on sunny slopes). Rock follows the landforms, so cliffs read as cliffs and hills as hills: some country is rocky, with bare rock on its upper flanks and ridge tops above a wobbling line, and drop walls and very steep ground are rock anywhere, each face one of a few grey tones. Snow lingers longest in hollows, and in the snow wind-scoured crests take a packed, icy tint while hollows hold bright drifts. The air warms with the biome: from the thaw on, the wintry blue sky and fog shift to a deeper summer blue that comes in lower, above a light blue haze, and distance fades the landscape (its colours drain a little and soften) instead of whitening it. Everywhere the haze stays clear around the car and builds gently with distance, so far hills read lighter but keep their colours, and glows warmer and brighter toward the sun (the sky glows to match at the horizon); the view reaches ≈1 km, the ground past the streamed chunks (≈250 m) carrying on as coarser far terrain with simple stand-ins for its trees (each standing where the real tree will), and only the last ≈200 m blend fully into the sky, into the sky's own colour in each direction so far hills melt into it. Spruces keep snow on their branches only where the ground is snowy; through the thaw broadleaf trees join them, then Scots pines, and from early in the thaw the trees gather into distinct woods with large open meadows between, a few small groups and the rare lone tree. Woods spread where the ground suits trees (moist lower slopes and hollows) and thin out on dry crests and ridge tops, but valley floors stay open meadow: a clear lane down the valley, framed by the woods on its flanks (a group or lone tree may stand there); nothing grows on rock. The country alternates along the descent between open stretches (meadows with a few small woods, groups and lone trees) and wooded stretches where the woods merge into forest round clearings: open runs often go on for well over a kilometre, wooded stretches for a few hundred metres, with wobbling edges across the slope (in the snow forest too). Now and then, every 8–10 km or so, comes a great forest: dense woods for half a kilometre to well over one and kilometres across, valley floors and all, the old trees standing as close as the young. Within a wood the trees are old and big in the core, standing wider apart there (gaps to thread between the big trunks), and young and small at a sparser fringe; lone trees are old. Each area is mostly one kind, sorted by the ground: spruce in moist, shaded hollows, pine on dry, high, sunny ground, broadleaf on low, sheltered slopes. Smaller detail is sparse and placed by the ground too: boulders lie about on bare rock and now and then on convex, sloping grass (in loose clusters, the odd one out in the open, snow-topped in the snow, never on drop walls or launch humps). They're solid, and the AI and traffic keep clear of them like trees. Each sits bedded in the ground until a hit is hard enough for its weight (rock density, so a 0.9 m stone weighs ≈0.7 t and the biggest ≈100 t): then it breaks loose and tumbles off with its share of the car's momentum (the car slows as in a collision with a free rock of that mass, and takes the impact in the same share), in a burst of dust and thrown clods of earth (torn turf among them in grass; chips of stone, a little darker than the rock, from rock ground) that stay where they land, leaving its imprint where it sat: the ground (looks and collider) is rebuilt finer there with its rounded underside pressed in, bare soil darker in the deep part, or in rock ground just the rock, darker deeper in. Nothing changes outside the boulder's footprint, so the hollow isn't seen appearing: the boulder uncovers it as it rolls off. Small stones go at a moderate head-on hit, mid-size ones take a fast one, the biggest never budge. Boulders are rounded all round and bedded with most of their underside in the ground. Loose stones are debris, cleaned up like car parts; the hollows stay. Shrubs gather along the edges of woods, and clumps of grass blades grow in patches on moist, green ground. Shrubs and grass are scenery only, with no collisions. Rooks live in the green country: some big broadleaf trees and every great oak hold a rookery (fewer pines and spruces, with only a few birds, never snowy ones), and any touch on the tree sends its rooks up and away from the car, climbing on fast wingbeats, then flapping and gliding off out of sight; an emptied tree stays empty. Flocks of 8–25 feed in fixed spots on open grass, walking, pecking and looking round, and lift off in a ripple away from a car within 22 m or a crash within 60 m. Now and then a loose flock crosses the sky, aimed across the player's way. B makes the next biome start just beyond the fog ahead (from the grassland, back toward snow): from there the biomes run on to where the next one begins, ramped over ≈400 m so it unfolds as you drive into it. Shift+B instead puts you straight at the next biome's start, facing downhill (from the grassland, back to the top). A treeline: bare snow at the top, then small sparse spruces that grow taller and denser further down (up to a cap), broken up by large open meadows. Every ~450 m there's a chance of a set piece spanning the slope: a steep drop, half of them with a ski-jump-style launch hump. A second, **varied** terrain preset is kept for later (dev: `?terrain=varied`): set pieces every ~380 m including huge drops, kickers with landing slopes, valleys and hills (climbs ≈30° at most), a swell along the fall line and bumpy/smooth stretches. |
| Car | One car: **70s two-door coupe** (soft, floaty, lots of body roll; all-wheel drive for arcade traction), built from a *blueprint*: profile curves and a side cross-section (tucked in at the bottom, widest at the hips, a shoulder crease and rounded top edge) a forward-leaning shark nose that forms a shallow V from above, with a recessed full-width surround under its beak holding the quad headlights and a barred grille, and a tail panel that leans in at the top, with two rounded taillights, each lens in two sections, a fuel filler cap between them license plates in recesses below both bumpers, and wrap-around chrome bumpers with two guards each (the rear one rising over the plate), traced from orthographic reference drawings (`ref/1970s-muscle-car/`, checked against the model with `scripts/overlay.mjs`), lofted into a body and split along the drawing's cut lines into hood, trunk, doors (with glass and mirrors), roof, glass and bumpers. Panels have real wall thickness (solidified, with edges along every cut line), and under them sit an engine bay (a low-poly V8 with a dark block, orange valve covers, a round air cleaner on its intake, a distributor behind it with plug wires running in a loom along the top of each valve cover and dropping over its edge to the plugs, pale headers curling down into the exhaust, and a belt drive with an alternator and a cooling fan up front; a chunky finned radiator with its hoses, the battery and a coolant tank beside it; on the firewall (painted on the bay side) the heater pipes the heater hoses run onto and the brake booster with its master cylinder and reservoir, brake lines running out to a proportioning valve; inner fender aprons sloping from the fenders down to the frame rails, a spring tower over each front wheel, and a radiator support; a power steering pump on the belt, battery cables, a vacuum hose and an overflow hose to the coolant tank; a low, wide washer reservoir strapped on the right apron; in a crash the radiator folds with the nose while the engine is shoved back whole), a cabin (bucket seats, rear bench, dashboard, steering wheel) and a spare wheel in the trunk. Under the floor runs a twin exhaust, traced from underside and rear drawings (`ref/1970s-muscle-car/`): a pipe from each manifold, through a muffler ahead of the rear axle, out to a chrome tip under each side of the rear bumper, kicking up over the rear axle. The rest of the underbody follows the underside drawing too (stretched to the car's proportions): frame rails and crossmembers under a ribbed floor, the oil pan, gearbox and driveshaft, a ribbed fuel tank on straps, and a live rear axle and front A-arms that follow the wheels' travel. The engine steams when the car is badly hurt and smokes when it dies. Colliders are derived from the blueprint. Cars are data-driven configs, so more can be added. |
| Physics | Custom raycast suspension (spring + damper per wheel, bump stops), arcade tire model (lateral grip, friction circle, handbrake slides; grip by the ground under the tyres: snow least, grass more, rock most), air levelling toward the flight path while S is held (an always-on mode exists in the car config). |
| Damage | Mesh **deformation** at impact points, plus **crumple zones** front and rear: hits squarely on the nose or tail push that end in (up to ~0.65 m at the front, ~0.45 m at the rear) – the hood or trunk lid tents up at a crease (as a ridge across its width, even over a tree's narrow notch), fenders bow out ahead of the wheel arches, the bumper bends, and the collision box shrinks with it, the car travelling on by the crush so the crumpled end stays against what it hit instead of stopping short and shrinking away from it. Trees punch a deep notch that the front wraps round (the skin can't stretch, so the corners, headlights and bumper ends are drawn in toward the notch); walls, ground, boulders and cars push in wide (boulders also throw sparks and stone dust, and crunch like a hard hit). Landing on the roof caves it in toward the beltline (up to ~0.25 m; the roof is the body's weak spot, so it gives way at lower impact than the chassis), leaning it over on a corner hit, bowing the pillars and shattering all the windows; the cabin's collision box comes down with it. Crush doesn't change handling. Panels break loose onto **physics hinges** first – doors swing out, hood and trunk lid open up, bumpers dangle from one end (a panel that breaks loose mid-crash still goes in with the body for the rest of that crash, so a hood never overhangs the crushed nose); airflow shuts doors and flips hoods up at speed – then tear off from more damage or violent flapping. Glass panes (windshield, rear, quarter and door windows) are see-through, crack in a spiderweb around the hit point (hard crashes carry through the body, so a big frontal hit can take out the windshield), then shatter into glinting shards that are thrown out from the pane, trail back on the air at speed and lie sparkling on the snow; a door window goes with its door. Head-, tail- and marker lights are rigid fixtures that ride on the crushed or dented body, following it at both ends (so a wide taillight tilts with a crumpled tail): hits smash the lens (it goes dark and sheds coloured shards), harder hits or deep crush pop the light out of its socket (headlights leave their place in the nose's recessed surround, taillights a real opening with a dark recess in the tail panel): some snap clean off (the harder they're knocked out, the likelier), the rest dangle on their wire, kept outside the body and bumpers, until a hard yank (a heavy landing) or more hits snap the wire. Small trim gets knocked clean off as tumbling debris: mirrors (they ride on the doors), the grille (baring the hole into the engine bay at the back of the surround), hubcaps, which pop off on hard knocks or landings and roll away, baring the steel wheel and its lug nuts, and the chrome exhaust tips, baring the pipe's open end, the license plates (leaving their dark recesses) and the fuel filler cap. Wheels break off. Losing a wheel changes handling. Coolant and oil leak independently, each until its tank runs dry: a damaged radiator (hits on the nose, front crush) leaves a green trail, a holed sump (hits from below, hard landings, a wrecked engine) a dark one; drops get denser and bigger the worse the leak and stretch into splashes at speed, and a dead car that has stopped bleeds its remaining oil into a spreading puddle. Low integrity kills the engine; landings (the ground hitting the underside) cost a third of what a crash does and can't take integrity into the red on their own, so they only finish off an engine crashes have worn down, and they knock the wheels less. Press N for a new car. |
| Camera | C cycles cameras. The default orbit camera trails the car (following its direction of travel, stays above the snow, widens its field of view with speed); drag the mouse to swing it freely around the car, from down at the snow (well below the car when it's in the air) to almost overhead, and scroll to zoom; where the snow is in the way it climbs its orbit, keeping its distance from the car. It holds the view where it's left; while the car is moving it eases back behind it after a few seconds. The cinematic helicopter cam hovers off to the side, slowly orbits and drifts in distance and elevation, lags with a telephoto lens and cuts to a new angle when the car gets away; drag to swing it around and tilt it (from down at the snow, looking up at the car, to steeply overhead), scroll to zoom (past ≈160 m, where the car shrinks to a speck, zooming out widens the lens instead); its automatic drift pauses while you steer. Both cameras move by angle and distance around the car, so swinging round follows the circle, and both keep the car in sight: when a crest or bump would block the view, they rise quickly and sink back slowly, and when trees hide most of the car they dissolve in front of it (a tree covering only a corner or a side stays whole; photo mode shows trees whole). **Photo mode** (from the pause, a right-drag, Shift+drag or Option+drag) hands over a free camera and hides the UI: drag orbits the point under the cursor, right-drag (or Option+drag) turns the camera the way the cursor goes, Shift+drag pans (keeping the grabbed point under the mouse, as if no further off than the car), scroll moves towards the point under the cursor and Shift+scroll zooms the lens; the arrow keys pan and move forward and back, Shift+arrows look round. Every move eases in. The camera stays 30 cm above the ground, and an orbit that meets it slides along it (or stops, where the ground rises steeply). Esc goes back to the pause; in slow motion it plays the action on, seen from the photo camera. A hidden extra, off for now (dev: `?section`), lets the camera go under the ground, which then shows cut open: turf, soil layers and stones. |
| Juice | A lens flare from the sun when the camera looks toward it (a starburst, a faint rainbow halo, a thin horizontal streak and fringed ghosts; hidden behind terrain, trees and cars), clear, see-through glass shards that mirror the sky and flash in the sun as they tumble, spray from the tyres (more when sliding) and puffs on landings and impacts that match the ground (snow powder, torn turf and soil, a brown dirt cloud, grey stone dust and chips), snow, leaves or needles shaken off trees depending on the tree, orange sparks from metal on rock or metal, screen shake and a camera dip on hard hits and landings, and looking into the sun (unless a ridge hides it) darkens the land and the car toward a silhouette against the sky. |
| AI traffic | Up to 4 AI cars come and go around the player, each with its own cruise speed. They spawn and despawn only out of the camera's sight (never in front of you). The AI plans its route far ahead for the open way through or round forest, off rock faces and tipping slopes, sets its speed by the grip and goes for a cairn ahead to knock it over; when stuck it backs out and drives away round trunks and steep rock, avoids the heading that got it stuck and picks its way to open ground in dense forest, and a reset puts a car on the nearest spot clear of trunks. P hands the player's car to the same AI (autopilot), which asks for a new car once it lies wrecked. |
| Controls | Keyboard: W/S or ↑/↓ for throttle and brake/reverse, A/D or ←/→ to steer, Space for handbrake, R to reset upright (keeps damage), N for a new car, B to start the next biome ahead (Shift+B to go straight there), Esc to pause (everything freezes, but the cameras can still be orbited and zoomed, and they hold the view where it's left; photo mode from there, see Camera), T for slow motion (eases in; ' cycles ½, ¼, ⅛ and 1/16 speed, starting at ¼; sound drops in pitch and muffles; the cameras stay on real time for orbiting). Dev mode (`?dev` in the address in any build, or typing "dev" within 0.7 s while playing; typing it again leaves and puts the game back as it plays): I and Shift+I step through terrain inspection views that colour the ground by what shapes it (landforms: valley floors, flanks, ledges, gullies, ridge tops and 5 m contours; layers: moguls, kicker ridges, drops and humps; steepness bands; moisture; sun exposure; curvature), also while paused; V looks straight down on the car (scroll zooms round the cursor, from 5 m to 1100 m up, drag pans, no haze); Shift+V draws a 2D map 4 km across round the view, in its own inspection view (or ground cover), with hill shading from the game's sun, the trees, boulders and the loaded chunks; G puts a rook flock ahead to drive into, Shift+G one startled right away; Shift+M silences the engines; F sets off a blast under the car, left and right by turns; J asks for a distance downhill and across and puts the car there, updating the address (`?start=` and `?x=`, which spawn there). In the air: hold S to level the car toward its flight path (otherwise free physics), A/D roll (W does nothing, since it's usually held). |

Small glass cards list the controls, with keys named for a Mac or a PC, one at a time in the bottom left corner: the driving controls, with Esc set apart as the way into pause and photo mode, or in photo mode only photo mode's. | Audio | Synthesised live with the Web Audio API, positional: V8 engine (combustion pulses from each cylinder bank into its own exhaust pipe, so it burbles like a cross-plane muscle car, a lumpy-cam idle that surges and stumbles, a dark muffled tone that opens up under throttle; pitch from RPM through simulated gears), tyres by ground (rolling, sliding, and a squeal on stone; loudness from the load on the tyres, the squeal's pitch falling with load, wilder slides harsher), body scrape by surface (metal grinding on rock, trees and cars; dragging over grass, snow and dirt), wind, plus one-shots for impacts, glass cracking/shattering, panels clanking loose and landings, and rocks: a knock as one breaks loose, then clacks as loose stones strike each other and thuds as they land. ## Later (not v0.1)

- More cars: pickup truck, dune buggy, compact hatchback (weight, suspension, grip and
  toughness differ per car).
- More biomes, including biomes that change along the descent (snow → forest → grassland).
- Gamepad support (analog steering and throttle).
- Goals: trick/airtime scoring, runs with a finish line.
- Juice: snow spray, sparks, smoke and fire, screen shake, audio (engine, crunch, landing), torn turf: tyres sliding hard or skidding locked cut ragged ruts in grass and hard hits gouge small holes where the body touches (a soil floor below raised turf lips), each hole throwing out a matching sod that lands flat and stays; only grass and dirt tear.
- Post-processing (ambient occlusion, bloom), additional camera modes.
- Smashable props (trees that snap, fences).

## Technical notes

- The first frame draws one invisible glass shard, so the shards' transmission pass (an extra render of the scene with its own shader variants) compiles while the game starts, not on the first crash that breaks a window.
- Fixed 60 Hz physics step with render interpolation (cars, hinged panels and debris). Slow motion feeds the step accumulator less time instead of shrinking the step, since damage, crush and the light wires are tuned per step.
- The fog is three.js's own, patched at startup into a gentle haze (by true distance, glowing toward the sun, draining colour a little in warm biomes): see `src/render/fogFade.ts` for what the patch touches and what to check on a three.js upgrade.
- Terrain height is an analytic function `h(x, z)`, so props, camera clamping and spawning can
  all query it without raycasts.
  `TerrainField.landform(x, z)` is the smooth part (slope, valleys, gullies, ledges, knolls) without moguls,
  kicker ridges or drops. `node scripts/terrain-map.mjs` draws relief maps with steepness stats.
- Terrain colliders are trimeshes built from the same vertices as the render mesh.
- Past the streamed chunks the far view (`src/world/farTerrain.ts`) draws the ground as coarse tiles (8 m grid, no colliders or props) out past the fog's end; it sits a little lower and is cut away wherever a chunk is loaded, so the detailed ground always covers it. Its trees are simple stand-ins placed by the tree layout itself (worked out ≈2 ms a frame, nearest tile first), each hidden once the chunk its trunk stands in is loaded and draws the real tree. Tiles and their trees are built only in frames that build no chunks.
- Ground cover, trees and boulders read the ground's environment (slope, curvature, moisture…) at the 2 m mesh grid nodes: a chunk samples it once for its whole grid, and trees and boulders take the nearest node's values from it (or work out that node alone when the AI looks outside loaded chunks), so they come out the same either way.
- Impact detection uses Rapier contact-force events on the chassis. Damage scales with the
  impact's Δv above a per-car toughness threshold.
